Applies to the hosted Hisar service at api.tryhisar.com, this website, the Hisar apps for macOS and iPhone, the hisar command-line tool and the Hisar browser extension.

Summary

  • Hisar is an end-to-end encrypted password, SSH key and secrets manager for developers. Your passwords, notes, keys and other items are encrypted on your devices before they are sent to our server. We cannot read them.
  • To run the service, the server does store some things it can read: your email address and name, your devices' names, who you share vaults with, timestamps, sizes, and IP addresses in a security log kept for one year. This page lists all of it.
  • There is no analytics, advertising, tracking or selling of data. Our pages and emails contain no trackers and the service sets no cookies.

Who runs Hisar

The hosted service is operated by Alper Berber, in the Netherlands ("we", "us"). We are the controller of the personal data described here. Questions and requests: {{ support_email_link }}.

What we cannot see

Your apps encrypt everything below with keys that never leave your devices. The server stores only the encrypted result, so neither we nor anyone who obtains a copy of our database can read it:

  • your master password and your Secret Key (signing in uses a protocol, OPAQUE, in which the server never receives the password);
  • the contents of your items: titles, usernames, passwords, websites, notes, custom fields, one-time-password secrets, passkeys and SSH private keys;
  • vault names;
  • what a share link contains, and the key or passphrase that opens it;
  • what an approval request from your Mac to your iPhone describes, and your answer.

Because we never have your master password or Secret Key, we cannot reset your password or recover your data. If you lose both your master password and every signed-in device and your Emergency Kit, your data is lost.

Kept only in memory

  • Rate limits. To slow down password guessing and spam, the server counts requests per IP address and per account in memory. These counters are never written to disk and are lost when the server restarts.
  • Approvals from your iPhone. When your Mac asks your iPhone to approve an SSH or command-line request, the server relays an encrypted request and an encrypted answer. It sees your account, which device asked, an ID, the sizes and the times, for at most a few minutes, in memory only.
  • Live updates. While your apps are open, the server keeps a connection to tell them that something changed, without content.

Logs and backups

  • Like most web services, our web server records each request (IP address, time, address requested, response status and browser or app version) in access logs, kept for 14 days. Opening a share link is recorded there the same way; the part of the link that holds the key is never sent to the server.
  • We back up the database every night. Backups are encrypted and kept for 30 days, so deleted data disappears from backups after that time.

Email

The server sends a few plain-text emails, with no images or tracking: an invitation when someone invites you to Hisar or shares a vault with you, and a note to you when someone accepted your invitation to a vault but your Hisar has not finished sharing it yet. Emails contain the names and email addresses of the people involved and, for invitations, a sign-up link; they never contain vault names or items. Email is not end-to-end encrypted. We send it through Google (Google Workspace), which processes the recipient's address and the message to deliver it.

Payments

Payments are handled by Lemon Squeezy, our reseller and merchant of record. When you buy Pro, you buy from them: they collect your payment details, billing address and VAT information under their own privacy policy. We receive your plan, its status, your country and an order reference, never your card number. We keep billing records as long as tax law requires.

Who else receives data

  • The people you share with see your name, your email address and your security code, and the vaults you share with them. Nobody else can look up whether an email address has a Hisar account; the service never lists its users.
  • Service providers that process data on our behalf: Hetzner Online GmbH runs the server and stores the database and backups, in Helsinki, Finland (EU); Google (Google Workspace) delivers email; Lemon Squeezy, our reseller and merchant of record, handles payments.
  • Authorities, only when the law requires it. What we could hand over is what this page lists; we cannot decrypt your vaults.

Google and Lemon Squeezy are based in the United States. Transfers of personal data to them are covered by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

We do not sell or rent personal data, and we do not use it for advertising or profiling.

The apps

  • The macOS and iPhone apps keep an encrypted copy of your vaults on the device so they work offline. Touch ID and Face ID are handled by Apple's operating system; Hisar never receives your fingerprint or face data.
  • The apps contact only the Hisar server you sign in to. The macOS app checks that server for updates while you are signed in.
  • The Hisar browser extension talks only to the Hisar app on the same computer, through the browser's native messaging, and sends nothing to the internet. It reads the address of the page you are on and the login forms on it, to offer matching logins and to offer saving a login after you sign in. Page contents are not stored or sent anywhere except to the Hisar app on your computer. It stores a random installation ID, the pairing token for your Hisar app, its settings, and the sites you chose "Never for this site" for, in the browser's local extension storage. Logins are filled only on the site they were saved for.

Security, and its limits

Traffic is encrypted with TLS. Items are encrypted with keys derived from your master password and your 128-bit Secret Key, so a copy of our database is not enough to guess your password offline. We work to keep the service secure, but please know the limits of any server-based design:

  • The server sees the metadata listed above: who you share with, how many items you have, how big they are and when they change.
  • A compromised server could try to hand you someone else's public key when you share a vault. Comparing security codes with the other person once (Settings → People) detects that.
  • The web page that opens a share link is served by our server, so a compromised server could change it. People who use the hisar command-line tool can open share links without the page.

If we learn of a breach that affects your personal data, we will tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

Legal bases

Where data protection law such as the GDPR applies, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Performance of a contract: account, device, vault and sharing data, and billing status, to provide the service you asked for; emails because you or someone sharing with you asked us to send them.
  • Legitimate interests: the security log, access logs and rate limits, to protect the service and its users against abuse and attacks.
  • Legal obligation: billing records we must keep for tax law.
  • Consent: only where we ask you for it. Today we ask for none, because there is no analytics or tracking.

  • You have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or the data protection authority in the EU country where you live or work.
