Security model

Your keys are made on your devices. Our server only ever holds ciphertext.

This page explains what Hisar protects, what our server can see, and where the limits are.

  1. On your deviceMaster password + 128-bit Secret Key → Argon2id and HKDF → your account unlock key. It decrypts your keyset: a symmetric key, a hybrid post-quantum key pair and a signing key.
  2. On the wireTLS, and sign-in with OPAQUE: the server never receives your password or anything it could guess it from without the Secret Key.
  3. On api.tryhisar.comEncrypted items, encrypted vault keys wrapped to each member, and the metadata below. No keys to open them.

What our server sees

  • Your email, and the emails you invite or share with
  • Which accounts can open which vaults
  • How many items a vault has, their sizes and when they change
  • Your devices, IP addresses and app versions
  • For share links: who made one, its size, expiry, view limit, views, and the IPs that open it
  • Your plan and billing status

What it never sees

  • Passwords, notes, one-time code seeds, SSH private keys
  • Item titles, URLs and vault names
  • Your master password or Secret Key
  • The key in a share link (it stays after the #)
  • What you approved or denied on your iPhone

Cryptography

PurposePrimitive
Password stretchingArgon2id, 64 MiB, parameters checked by the client
Key derivationHKDF-SHA256
ItemsXChaCha20-Poly1305
Vault keys between people and devicesHPKE (RFC 9180), hybrid ML-KEM-768 + X25519
Sign-inOPAQUE (RFC 9807), ristretto255
Signatures (approvals, keysets)Ed25519
Share links, decrypted in the browserAES-256-GCM, PBKDF2 for the passphrase

The hybrid KEM stays secure as long as either half holds, so recorded traffic and database copies stay safe against a future quantum computer.

Threat model

If someone…What happens
Copies our databaseThey get ciphertext. Guessing your password offline is impossible without your Secret Key.
Controls our serverThey can withhold or delay sync and approvals, and see the metadata above. They can't read items, forge an approval or weaken your password stretching.
Watches the networkTLS and OPAQUE: nothing to learn, no one to impersonate.
Runs code on your unlocked MacOut of scope for any password manager. Hisar narrows it: every key use asks and names the app, and unsigned programs are flagged.
Is a coding agent you approvedIt only sees vaults you made available. A command you approve has real values and could leak them; masking hides values in output, it isn't a sandbox. get_secret puts a value in the agent's transcript.
Steals your iPhoneIt needs Face ID to approve, and an approval never unlocks your Mac. Sign the phone out from another of your devices.

No recovery, by design

We can't reset your master password or show your Secret Key. Print your Emergency Kit when you sign up, and keep a second device signed in.
